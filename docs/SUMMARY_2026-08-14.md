# Where the MR study stands

**14 August 2026.** Built from `docs/evidence/FINDINGS.json` (harvested by
`scripts/collect_findings.py`), not from notes. Every number below carries its verification
status; anything marked unverified is not a result.

---

## 1. The one-sentence version

Microrefrigeration's value is set by **thermal degeneracy** — how many blocks sit within the
device's lift of the peak — and by **how much operating margin the part is specified to hold**.
Both are properties of the die and the system spec, not of the cooling device. On the die we
have been simulating, both are unfavourable, and neither is fixed by a better MR device.

---

## 2. What is measured and verified

### 2.1 The degeneracy result (the mechanism behind everything else)

`examples/thermal_tiers.py`, 34-core 7 nm, 1.00 W/mm², 88 CFM:

| design / activity | plateau | clip-one gain | % of a 10 K device |
|---|---|---|---|
| balanced core, all cores active | 15 | 1.25 K | 12% |
| mixed, 25% of cores active | 6 | 2.42 K | 24% |
| single-core turbo | 7 | 2.56 K | 26% |
| concentrated core (FPU ×4) | 6 | 5.44 K | 54% |
| **concentrated + turbo** | **2** | **9.86 K** | **99%** |
| concentrated ×2 (partial) | 29 | 0.21 K | 2% |

Two independent degeneracies stack: **inter-core** (N copies of a unit, broken by activity) and
**intra-core** (the hot functional units within a core, broken only by concentrating power into
one structure). They compose — together they leave a genuinely non-degenerate peak.

**Partial concentration is worse than none** (×2: plateau 29, gain 0.21 K): raising a unit to
*meet* the existing hot ones rather than pass them maximises the crowd.

### 2.2 `dt_max` cannot fix a degenerate peak (design F)

| die | dt_max 10 K | 20 K | 30 K | blocks for full lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| balanced | 1.25 K | 1.25 K | 1.25 K | 15 → 120 → **785** |
| concentrated + turbo | 9.86 K | 9.86 K | 9.86 K | 2 → 23 → 46 |

**Clip-one gain never moves with `dt_max` on either die.** It is the gap to the next block — a
die property. `dt_max` only decides whether the device can *reach* that gap. A 10 K device is
already matched to the concentrated die (gap 9.86 K) and 8× oversized for the balanced one
(gap 1.25 K). *A better MR device is worth nothing on a degenerate peak.*

### 2.3 The rescue cost is a curve in the margin demanded — with a knee

34-core at 1.10 W/mm², 88 CFM. The bare die has **no steady state**; MR gives it one:

| MR target | peak °C | heat removed | MR electrical | blocks cooled | verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 93 °C | 92.98 | 19.83 W | 34.40 W | 1126 | yes |
| 94 °C | 96.00 | 1.82 W | 3.18 W | 24 | yes |
| 95 °C | 96.82 | 0.254 W | 0.44 W | 5 | no |
| 96 °C | 96.28 | 0.298 W | 0.52 W | 5 | yes |
| 97 °C | 97.02 | 0.247 W | 0.43 W | 4 | yes |
| 98 °C | 99.94 | 0.169 W | 0.29 W | 4 | no |
| 99 °C | 100.98 | 0.145 W | 0.25 W | 4 | no |

**The knee is at ~94 °C and the cost falls 80× between 93 and 97 °C** (34.40 W → 0.43 W, both
verified). The knee is the plateau: below ~94 °C the target dips into the 15-block crowd and MR
must cool the whole die (1126 blocks); above it, only the 4–5 blocks that actually set the peak.

**This ties 2.1 and 2.3 together — the degeneracy is why the margin is expensive.** It is also
the largest lever found in the project: 4 K of specified margin costs 80× the cooling power.

The 99 °C row lands at 100.98 °C, past the logic's 100 °C limit, so the usable window closes
around 97–98 °C.

### 2.4 MR as a clock enabler (design A)

Single-core turbo clock search, MR target 2 K under the limit:

| design | no MR | with MR | gain | MR electrical |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| balanced | 4.812 GHz | 4.812 GHz | **+0.0%** | 0.01 W |
| concentrated ×4 | 4.719 GHz | **5.000 GHz** | **+6.0%** | 9.01 W |

On a balanced many-core MR has nothing to clip. On a concentrated core it buys ≥6% — and the
run stops at the V/F table's ceiling, not at a thermal limit.

### 2.5 Cooling alone, and where the cliff is

* 50× better sink resistance (1.0 → 0.02 K/W) buys **+55% clock** and never reaches the rated
  5.0 GHz; the last 2.5× buys 1.1%.
* Above R_th 0.1 K/W the part never reaches its 100 °C spec limit — it **runs away first**, at
  85.7 °C. The leakage instability, not the temperature spec, caps a poorly-cooled die.
* Verified no-MR cliff (34-core, 88 CFM): between **1.05 and 1.10 W/mm²**.
* 128-core at 0.80 W/mm²: 88.6 °C, MR idle (nothing above target) — verified.

### 2.6 Stacked memory does not move the constraint (design D)

| stack | binding | logic peak | memory peak | memory margin | memory plateau |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 die, air | LOGIC | 89.5 °C | 61.0 °C | 34.0 K | 16 of 16 |
| 1 die, liquid | LOGIC | 86.7 °C | 57.8 °C | 37.2 K | 16 of 16 |
| 1 die, concentrated logic | LOGIC | 90.8 °C | 62.7 °C | 32.3 K | 16 of 16 |
| **4 dies, air** | **LOGIC** | 90.7 °C | 63.0 °C | 32.0 K | 64 of 64 |
| 8 dies | *running* | | | | |

The memory runs ~28 K cooler than the logic with a *complete* plateau, and adding depth has not
changed that yet. The logic peak is a **local** constriction hotspot; its heat spreads laterally
before crossing into the memory, so the memory sees die-average flux. It runs cool **and**
averages the hotspots away.

DRAM parameters here are JEDEC conventions, not datasheet extractions — this answers "does the
constraint move", not "how much memory power".

## 4. The verification rule is now too strict, and it is hiding results

19 of 99 harvested entries are flagged unquotable, and the pattern is systematic: typically
**1 of 13–15 solves** in a point fails, with a worst spread of 7–20 K, while the *reported*
solve is converged to 0.001–0.01 K.

That is expected behaviour, not a defect. The envelope-anchored MR loop **deliberately probes
past the stability boundary** to bracket the minimum plan — those probes are marginal states and
are exactly the solves that fail verification. Flagging the whole point because an exploratory
probe was unverified conflates "we tested a state that turned out to be unstable" with "the
answer we report is unverified".

Most visibly, all six pixel-pitch runs are flagged, and they agree to within 0.02 W across
1 µm / 10 µm / 100 µm and both policies — which is itself the answer (spot size is not the
binding constraint), currently unquotable on a technicality.

**Fix:** `unconverged` should mean *the solve that produced the reported result failed
verification*; exploratory probes should be counted separately. This must not weaken the guard —
it caught three real errors today — so the reported-state check stays exactly as strict.

## 5. Proposed next steps, in order

1. **Split verification status (half a day, unblocks ~15 results).** As §4. Re-harvest; the
   pitch, margin and 128-core answers likely become quotable without new runs.
2. **Rebuild V/F from IRDS (half a day).** The one place absolute numbers are known wrong. Fixes
   the clock ceiling, the power-vs-clock shape, and probably *raises* MR's measured clock gain.
3. **Finish the 8-high stack, then decide design D's fate.** If depth still does not move the
   constraint, D is answered negatively and the memory case rests on 2.5D or on much higher
   memory activity, both of which are different studies.
4. **Margin curve at a second density and cooling class.** The knee at ~94 °C is the biggest
   commercial lever found; it is currently one curve at one operating point.
5. **A real accelerator floorplan** (the `die_images` set) to replace the power-redistribution
   proxy behind the concentrated-core result — the strongest MR case rests on a proxy.
6. **Pulsed / transient MR — blocked on a workload, not on modelling.** Our trace is 3.2 ms
   (16 × 0.2 ms) and its burstiness is in LoadQ/TLB/ICache/IMC, while the *hot* units are flat
   (median unit swing 0.0%). Pre-cooling lead time must match τ ≈ 2.2 ms, not the 100 ms
   originally proposed. Needs a longer trace with phase behaviour in the compute units before
   any of it can pay.
