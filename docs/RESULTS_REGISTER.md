# Results register — what may be quoted, and what may not

**Single source of truth for every number this project has produced.** Last updated 19 September 2026 (§P0.37 the two-sink gen-3 stack; §P0.38 the designed finger cluster; §P0.39 the rail model; earlier: §P0.29 the F2 costs corrected; §P0.30 the cooling-system ledger).

`[!]` **Read this before quoting any figure, in a proposal, a paper, a slide, or a session summary.**
The project has produced results for six weeks and has withdrawn a substantial fraction of them.
The withdrawn ones are the ones most likely to resurface, because they live on in older drafts,
earlier artifacts, and the reasoning of anyone who read the repository a fortnight ago.

**How to use this file**

- A claim in **§1 (quotable)** may be used as written, *with its caveat*. The caveat column is not
  editorial trimming — every one of them has bitten at least once.
- A claim in **§2 (withdrawn)** must not be used. Each row names what to say instead.
- A claim in **§3 (open)** has not been measured. Do not derive it, do not estimate it, and do not
  let it into a document — ask, or measure it.
- If a figure you want is in none of the three sections, **it has probably not been measured on this
  die under the current accounting.** Treat that as a "no" until checked.

`[+]` **The evidence for §1 and §2 is materialised as two directories at the repository root**, so a
proposal writer never has to pick the good files out of the working set:

| directory | holds | per-item note |
|---|---|---|
| **`results-quotable/`** | evidence behind the §1 claims below | `SOURCE.md` — claim, figure, reproduce command, caveat |
| **`results-historical/`** | evidence behind the §2 withdrawn claims | `WHY_RETIRED.md` — what to say instead, why it failed, **what still survives inside** |

Evidence JSONs are copied (small, so a directory is a self-contained snapshot); the multi-gigabyte
solve trees are symlinked. Rebuild or drift-check with
`python scripts/build_results_registers.py [--verify]`.
`[!]` They are **curated, not exhaustive** — `docs/evidence/` remains the full ~110-file working
set, and a file's absence from either directory means nobody has classified it, not that it is
wrong.

## 4. Standing constraints that bound everything above

- **One die as the reference.** The one-die rule was lifted 9 Sep for the architecture-evolution
  phase; the 34-core 7 nm skylake stays the fixed reference and nothing generalises across
  floorplans without re-measurement (D1, D4 are the first).
- **`[!]` Past ~8× cluster density, shape is a design variable** (§P0.36, §P0.38): compact is cheaper
  and a rung higher to ~8×; at 16× / 32× the DESIGNED finger layout holds 243 W where the tiler's
  strips hold 202 / 162 W and the compact block 121 / 111 W. Quote a dense-cluster row with its
  unit SHAPE — compact, strip (the tiler's) or designed finger — never "the 16× cluster".
- **`[!]` The caches' leakage is an HP-device input** (§P0.40): 306× lower with LSTP cells. Quote cache
  RATIOS and thermal geometry; a cache-leakage saving in watts carries its CELL CLASS. Do not say a cold memory zone has no
  benefit either — that is unmeasured beyond two points on one die (§P0.42).
- **`[!]` A throughput claim names its memory arrangement** (§P0.43): elasticity to clock at 4 / 16 GHz is
  0.78 / 0.47 on interposer memory and 0.51 / 0.21 on off-package DDR (FFT); F1c's +14 % clock is
  +11 % instructions/s on the former and ≈ +7 % on the latter.
- **`[!]` The IPC(f) behind every throughput claim is on HBM-class (2.5D) memory** (§P0.40): FFT's
  t = 0.328/f + 0.0191 ns per instruction. Say which memory arrangement an IPC(f) came from.
- **`[!]` The cache zone is `'^(L2|L3)(_[0-9]+)?$'`, not the default `'^(L2|L3)'`** (§P0.37): the default
  also matches the 34 `L2Pred_*` branch-predictor blocks; `'^(L2|L3)_[0-9]'` starves the ledger of
  the bridged `L3` aggregate. The default is unchanged so D3 / X4 reproduce (their plans did not
  move under the correction; their zone MAX did). Pass the pattern on any cache-objective run.
- **`[!]` A storage die behind an isolating interface needs a far-face heat path to EXIST unpowered**
  (§P0.37): adiabatic, or ≥ 10 K/W, and the idle arm diverges. A two-sink row without its
  `array_idle` arm holding is not a design. There is no control arm on the two-sink stack.
- **`[!]` A memory die's temperature is not the design variable beside the cooled die — its HEAT PATH
  is** (§P0.44): in every stacked placement the DRAM banks sit at the die's area-average temperature,
  25–45 K under the hot spot, and the 85 °C line never binds in a held row. Quote the cost as what
  the memory does to the compute die's path (sink-side: the stack's series resistance, +0 for one
  hybrid-bonded die, +40 W or no hold for 8-high) or what the compute die's cooler does for the
  memory (far-face: −6 to +16 W by the far-face sink). The storage array's watts on a far-face
  row are the one-scalar envelope's share unless the banks are above target — say which.
- **`[!]` A cold cache's performance lever through hit latency is nil on the recorded kernels** (§P0.45):
  ±50 % of L2 / L3 access time moves CoMeT's FFT and lu.cont by < 0.5 %. A cold-zone benefit, where
  one exists, is a LEAKAGE benefit; do not derive a throughput gain for a faster cooled SRAM from
  these kernels. The stack-side ledger above 1.00 W/mm² is planner-limited (§3), not measured.
- **`[!]` The isolation's role reverses between a cache die and a DRAM die** (§P0.37 vs §P0.44): the
  cache die at 280 K wants ≤ 0.1 W/mK; the DRAM die at 80 °C wants the via field's ~0.85 W/mK and
  has no steady state behind an aerogel layer with a 10 K/W far-face path (refresh runaway). A
  signalled memory die's interface is the via field's conductance; quote k_eff with the via count.
- **`[!]` On a densified cluster the film's capability DOES bind** (§P0.32): the 8× / 16× clusters at
  100 µm pitch ask 158–172 W/mm² of a tile driven to 253–258 K on held rows and lose the top rung
  with tiles capped by the dye's cold end (190–198 W/mm² asked at 253–254 K). The reference-die
  statement below holds for the reference die.
- **Steady per-tile demand never exceeds ~5 W/mm² on any die in this repository** at 500 µm pitch
  (41 W/mm² only at quarter coverage on the CPU, §P0.18.2; 5.3 at 3.15 W/mm² on the GA100,
  §P0.24) against ≥ 813 W/mm² from the target device at 300 K tiles. The one exception is
  **transient**: the burst plan (§P0.25) asks up to 13 W/mm² of one tile for 10 ms at 1.00 W/mm² / 3×
  (5.2 at 0.80 / 2×), still 60× under the device.
  At 200 µm pitch the steady demand at 3.00 W/mm² is **14.8 W/mm²** (§P0.28, the highest steady
  demand recorded), 55× under the device. No claim may rest on the
  extractor's ideal design point being *needed*; what the headroom buys is pitch and margin.
- **Every rung above ~1.3 W/mm² is a current statement as well as a thermal one** (§P0.27): at fixed
  clock and 0.70 V the die draws 1.54× the native rail current at 1.20 W/mm², 2.45× at 2.00, 2.88×
  at 2.40, and the dense cluster's cALU 2× / 4× that. Quote `J / J_native` with any rescue rung
  used as an operating point, and any EM lifetime with `n` and `E_a`. The reliability dividend of
  cooling is a matched-POWER statement (2.4× at 1.10); at matched density the laser's worst block
  ages 12–50× faster than the native die's.
- **Every throughput claim carries IPC(f)** (§P0.33): the FLOP/s proxy moves only with the clock;
  on CoMeT's FFT kernel the elasticity of instructions per second to clock is **0.78–0.84 at
  3.3–4.9 GHz** (0.47 on lu.cont, 0.84 on swaptions from 8 to 16 GHz), so a "+x % clock" is
  ~0.8 x in throughput on a memory-touching kernel. Quote `f × IPC(f)` with the benchmark named.
- **`[!]` Above ~2× cluster density the RAILS bind before the cooler does** (§P0.39): on native-sized rails
  the 8× / 16× clusters clock 19–31 % low with the array's plan at 0 W. Quote a dense-cluster CLOCK
  with its rail assumption (`rail=off`, or the droop budget and `resize`), beside source and limiter.
- **Every clock carries its V/F source and its limiter** (§P0.31): `spice` is the gate-only
  ceiling (4.17 GHz), `fmax` the generalized period with a reliability-budgeted V_max at the
  solved temperature; the two differ by 8 % at the 92 °C target. Never quote a clock above the
  model's own ceiling, and never one whose limiter the row does not name.
- **Two control ceilings exist — say which.** The density probe's shaped arm (leakage a fixed
  fraction of every block's power; 34-core 0.60–0.65, 70-core 0.40–0.50) and the driver's control
  arm (McPAT's per-unit leakage split; 34-core 0.88–1.00, 70-core 0.70) differ by ~1.5–1.75×.
  §1.3's "0.60–0.65 shaped" is the probe's; the rescue and dark-silicon ladders sit on the
  driver's. A prediction written against the wrong one was falsified by that alone (§P0.23.2 P1).
- **Above ~127 °C report "non-viable", not a number.** McPAT refuses input outside 300–400 K and
  the leakage curve is extrapolated past its table.
- **Sub-ambient figures are a bracket, not a point.** ASAP7 is a *predictive* PDK and its GIDL
  coefficients are a model choice. Quote across both brackets.
- **An `unconverged` solve is neither a hold nor a failure.** It must never become a ceiling or a
  rescue. 4–20 rows per catalogue arm are in this state.
- **The floorplan-pack images are third-party subscriber content.** Numbers derived from them may
  be published; the plates may not.
- **MR *costs* are not comparable across leakage curves** unless normalised to a common peak — the
  minimum-plan descent stops wherever it first holds target. This has reversed a sign twice.
