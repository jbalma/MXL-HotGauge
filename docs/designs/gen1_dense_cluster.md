# Gen 1 design record — the dense execution cluster (D1)

**9 September 2026 (§P0.22.3).** The first architectural change made *because* of a measurement
(the arm-D rescue ladder, §P0.18.2) and re-measured against the reference die at matched die
watts. Status is in the scorecard at the end — rows land in `results/d1_family/` (control ladder)
and `results/d1_family_arr/` (array rungs) and are read by `examples/d1_family_report.py`.

## Why this and not another change

`EVOLUTION_LADDER.md` §2.2: the array frees the transistor-density term of (1.32) specifically —
it resolves a 142 × 115 µm unit at 200 µm pitch, its coverage is free, and the flux it is asked
for is 25× under the platform — while the clock is capped by the 100 °C spec and the V/F table
(`CLOCK_HEADROOM.md`) and voltage is not an architecture result. The conventional reason to
spread the cluster out is the 1.4× concentration penalty; the laser closes it (35/35 rescues).

## The constraint, before and after

- **Before (gen 0):** leakage runaway through `cALU` at 0.60–0.65 W/mm² on the reference die.
- **After (gen 1, measured on the family):** the laser holds the 2× denser cluster at every
  reference rung to 2.00 and the 4× denser one to 1.60; the cooler's cost is set by the
  cluster's hot spot at the low rungs (2.8–6.9× the reference's plan) and converges to ~1.2×
  where the plan is die-wide. **What binds next is the stability boundary** — the cool branch
  disappears one rung (×0.5) or two rungs (×0.25) below the reference's 2.40 — with the extractor
  never binding (0 tiles capped) and conservation not reached. At the low rungs the binding
  quantity is the **MR electrical budget**: 15–35 W of removal where the reference needs none.

## The constraint after gen 1, read from the fields (11 Sep, §P0.27)

The recorded final power maps of the family's holding rungs were re-solved once and read as
current densities (`EVOLUTION_LADDER.md` §2.3). At matched watts the ×0.5 member's cALU carries
**2.00×** the reference's current density at every rung (121 / 162 / 202 W: 3.05 / 4.07 / 5.08
against 1.54 / 2.05 / 2.56 of the native die's) and the ×0.25 member's **3.9×** — exactly the
area ratio, because the array clips both clusters to the same 92 °C landing; the die's total
current is the reference's. Under Black's law (n = 2, E_a = 0.9 eV, stated) the ×0.5 cluster's
worst block ages 43 / 96 / 143× faster than the native die's, the ×0.25's 196 / 423×; the
cluster's core-domain gradient is 1.19–1.26× (×0.5) and 1.64× (×0.25) the reference's, so
density costs skew as well as cooling watts. **What binds the dense cluster next is its rails**:
a 2× / 4× denser cluster needs 2× / 4× the stripe metal over it before it needs more light.
MEASURED as `J`; ARGUED as a limit. X3 (§P0.27.3) re-measures the ×0.5 member at the book's
70 % utilisation, where the cluster is 1.22× denser in silicon rather than 2×.

## At the book's utilisation (X3, 11 Sep, §P0.27.3)

Rebuilt at U = 70 %, T = 15 % and 20 µm cache halos (`d1_exec0.5_u70`, 121.5 mm²; the
utilisation control `d1_exec1_u70`, 137.7 mm²) and measured at matched watts on a 100 µm grid
beside the reference on the same grid: the cluster is **1.22× denser in silicon**, every rung to
243 W holds with 0 tiles capped, the plan is 0.75–0.90× the reference's (a larger, lower-density
die) and **1.16–1.44× the utilisation control's** at 162–243 W (9.8× at 121 W); the shaped
control cliff is the reference's rung (60.7 / 70.8 W) where utilisation alone had bought one
rung (70.8 / 80.9). `docs/evidence/x3_utilisation.json`; `EVOLUTION_LADDER.md` §2.4.
